# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **There are buyers with real budgets.** Two active B2B accounts bring in about 8,000 PLN a month together (fashion school about 3,200, scrubs maker about 4,800). One pending army supplier is worth about 12,000 PLN a month. Businesses that need fabric for production have money, a repeating need and high switching costs once a supplier is qualified. This is the best market the business touches.
- **Demand is visible and cheap to reach.** On Allegro, six listings took 247 orders from about 8,000 views in 30 days. Listings priced at or under the competitor convert at roughly 3.7 to 4.3% (Wiskoza, Jersey, Krepa). The two priced above the competitor (Aksamit, Szyfon) convert at about 2%. Buyers are easy to find.
- **Some of the stack already exists.** Cut to length from 0.5 m, a wide catalogue, OEKO-TEX paperwork for the medical buyer, and a sample-box idea that lowers the buyer's risk.

## What would kill it (top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It is a commodity that competes on price.** The Allegro data shows conversion following price. Nothing in the brief makes the offer different apart from being cheaper. With no known gross margin per meter and no known landed cost, nobody can say whether the cheapest listings make money. If they don't, more volume makes things worse.
2. **The buyer cannot trust that the order will arrive.** This is the biggest weakness in the value equation. A bestseller shows as "pre-order", two products cannot be added to cart, 3 of 8 tracked SKUs are at zero stock, and lead times are 50 to 70 days with a 20 Nov 2026 order deadline. For a B2B buyer running production, a stockout is the main pain they pay to avoid. Right now the business is causing that pain, not removing it.
3. **The B2B offer is not packaged.** Ordering is manual by phone and email. There are no wholesale terms, no guarantee and no service promise. The 12,000 PLN/month army account is still pending. The highest-value buyers are being sold the same thing as a hobby sewer, just in larger amounts.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is gross margin per meter after landed cost and shipping, by fabric? Is it different on Allegro and on B2B?
- What does it cost to win a customer on each channel, and how often do they buy again? Neither number is tracked yet.
- Why did the army supplier not close? Was it price, certification, lead time or proof of capacity?
- Could you guarantee stock for a B2B account, for example by reserving rolls for its next 3 months of orders? What would that cost in working capital?
- Could you raise B2B prices 15 to 20% in exchange for guaranteed availability, fixed lead times, certificates with every delivery and NET30, and still be the obvious choice?
- Are the B2C segment estimates (about 45,500 PLN a month in total) based on anything measured?
- Why sell this cheaply on Allegro when Wiskoza already costs 1.60 PLN less than the competitor and is the top seller?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Within 30 days: confirm margin per meter and cost per order** for the top 5 SKUs and each channel. No pricing or expansion decisions until then.
2. **Fix whatever stops people buying before spending on growth.** Make the broken products buyable, place the China order for A-items before 20 Nov 2026, and keep the paused Allegro listing paused until it has photos.
3. **Package one named B2B offer.** For example, a "production supply" program with reserved stock, a lead-time guarantee with a penalty or credit if it is missed, certificates included, NET30 and a sample kit for qualifying. Price it on reliability, not on being cheapest. Use it to close the pending army account and the two prospects.
4. **No new channels or brands** (Emag, Cdiscount, pet or electronics) until the B2B offer has at least one more paying account and margin is known.
