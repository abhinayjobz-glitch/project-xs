# Product lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Real demand exists where the experience works.** On Allegro, the listings that are priced well and in stock convert: Dzianina Jersey 62 orders from 1,450 views (about 4.3%), Wiskoza Czarna 87 from 2,340 (about 3.7%), Krepa Różowa 41 from 1,120 (about 3.7%). Where the experience is broken (velvet out of stock, cotton paused with no photos), conversion drops to about 1.5–2%. The product can sell, and the brief's own numbers show the cost of each broken seam.
- **Paying B2B customers who return.** A fashion school and a medical scrubs maker buy monthly. Repeat institutional buyers are a sign that the core item (the right fabric, cut correctly, delivered) is good enough.
- **A clear one-sentence product could exist:** "The right fabric, by the half meter, shipped fast from Warsaw." It is not being delivered consistently yet.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The front door is broken.** The bestselling velvet shows "pre-order" and can't be bought. Two products have no add-to-cart button. 17 country stores show only PLN and only Polish text. A German visitor's first five minutes are: they can't read the page, they can't see the price in their currency, and sometimes they can't buy at all. Every other idea depends on this working.
2. **No focus.** The idea list covers a B2B portal, Emag, Cdiscount, a sample box, TikTok, email, a logistics center, and new brands in pet products, electronics, hair care and fitness. With operations and customer service both on leave, the team can't do even the fabric business well right now. Spreading further will make every touchpoint worse.
3. **Hidden seams in fulfilment.** Stock-outs on 3 of 8 tracked SKUs, an order deadline of 20 Nov before Chinese New Year, and repeated courier overcharges are corners customers can't see yet. They show up as late parcels, "pre-order" labels and silence. The sloppiness behind the counter is already reaching the front of it.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what is TextilWorld the best in the world at, and for whom?
- Walk me through a customer's first five minutes on textilworld.pl from Germany. What do they see, and where do they leave?
- Which of the ideas on the list would you cut today? Which brand line would you never start?
- Where does an order pass between people or systems (Shopify, BaseLinker, Allegro, the warehouse, the courier), and which handoff fails most often?
- What does a customer receive after the parcel: a follow-up, a care note, a reason to come back? What is the repeat-purchase rate? (It is not known.)
- What is the gross margin per meter, and the shipping cost per order? Without these, nobody can tell whether a 39.90 PLN sample box or a 0.5 m order makes money. (Both are unknown.)
- Who owns the customer experience end to end while operations and customer service are both away?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Fix the front door before anything new:** every bestseller can be bought, local currency and translated pages in the top markets, no garbled text, photos on every listing. Measured by Allegro and Shopify conversion returning to the ~4% level the working listings already show.
2. **Cut the list in writing.** Drop the new brands and the logistics center from the plan. Pick one or two growth bets at most (for example, B2B reorders, or the sample box), and only after condition 1 is met.
3. **No bestseller goes to zero.** The A-items reorder is placed before 20 Nov 2026, and a named person owns stock-outs and customer communication end to end.
4. **Know the unit economics.** Report margin per meter and shipping cost per order before investing in any channel expansion.
