# The board on TextilWorld

**Vote: 3 × FUND IF. Average score 4.7 / 10** (Offers 5, Monopoly 4, Product 5).

All three lenses would back the business, but none would back growth spending until three things are fixed. Full memos are in `board/offers.md`, `board/monopoly.md` and `board/product.md`.

## Risks every member raised

1. **Nobody knows the margin.** Gross margin per meter, landed cost, shipping cost per order, CAC and repeat rate are all unknown. All three lenses made "know the unit economics" a condition. Until those numbers exist, no one can say whether the cheapest, best-selling Allegro listings make money.
2. **It's a commodity that competes on price.** In the Allegro sample, conversion follows price. Listings priced at or below the competitor convert at about 3.7–4.3%. Those priced above convert at about 2%. Nothing yet makes TextilWorld the obvious choice except being cheaper.
3. **Broken front door and stock-outs.** A bestseller shows as pre-order, two products have no buy button, 3 of 8 SKUs are at zero, all 17 storefronts show only PLN and Polish, and the China order is due by 20 Nov 2026. The business is causing the pain buyers pay to avoid: being unable to get the fabric when they need it.
4. **Too many ideas, too few people.** Emag, Cdiscount, a logistics center and pet, electronics, hair-care and fitness brands are all on the list. Meanwhile operations and customer service are both on leave.

## Where they disagreed

- **Offers** sees the best market in **B2B production buyers**: package a named "production supply" offer and price it on reliability, not on being cheapest.
- **Monopoly** agrees on B2B but goes further. Pick **one niche to dominate** (certified fabric for medical/workwear makers, or fashion-school supply) and build a real moat there: private label, guaranteed stock, contract terms.
- **Product** puts the **website and listings first**. Nothing new until the existing storefront converts at the ~4% the working listings already show.

## Conditions checklist (the rest of the pack tests these)

- [ ] Margin per meter and cost per order for the top 5 SKUs, per channel, within 30 days (`/founder-cfo`)
- [ ] Every bestseller buyable, with local currency and translated pages in the top markets and photos on every listing
- [ ] A-item China reorder placed before **20 Nov 2026**, with a named owner for stock-outs and customer communication
- [ ] One named B2B offer: reserved stock, a lead-time guarantee, certificates included, NET30. Use it to close the pending army account.
- [ ] Choose one niche to own, size it and name its competitors within 90 days (`/founder-competitors`)
- [ ] In writing: no new channels (Emag/Cdiscount) and no new brand categories until the above are done

## The strongest version the board can see

This differs from the CEO's list. The strongest TextilWorld is **not** a multi-category e-commerce group with its own logistics center. It is **the reliable fabric supplier for Polish production buyers**: fashion schools, scrubs and workwear makers, uniform suppliers and small clothing brands. They get guaranteed stock on core fabrics, certificates with every delivery and NET30 terms, and pay a bit more for it. Allegro and the Shopify store stay as the volume channels that find those buyers, once the front door works.
