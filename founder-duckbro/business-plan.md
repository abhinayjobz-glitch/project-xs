## The idea

- What it is: A pre-launch EU online brand (duckbro.store, "Est. 2026") selling humorous duck-themed socks, bags and accessories in limited "drops", made to order once enough people reserve.
- Who it is for: Per the site, "humans who don't take themselves too seriously". In practice: young adults in the EU and UK who like humorous, meme-adjacent fashion, plus people buying a fun gift.
- What it sells, at what price: 12 products. Socks: Quack Pack 5 pairs €29.99, Le Waddle €19.99, Pond Life €24.99, Crew Cuts 5 pairs €27.99. Bags: Duck Tote €22, Le Backpack 16L €55, Mini Crossbody €35, The Weekender 24L €64. Accessories: Le Cap €28, Duck Charm €9.99, Quack Scarf €34, Keyring Trio €14.99.
- Where and how: Online only (Next.js site, Stripe checkout). Ships from an EU warehouse to the EU and UK in 2–4 business days, €4 flat, free over €50. Everything is pre-order: €4.99 reserves an item, credited later, and production starts at 100 reservations ("Zero-Waste Pack"). 30-day returns. 10% off the first order for joining the email list. Instagram @duckbr0, TikTok @duck__br0, X @duckbr0.
- Budget and constraints: Unknown. Unit costs, supplier, MOQ, current reservations, ad budget and team size are not stated on the site.

**What it is:** duckbro is a pre-launch EU/UK online brand selling funny duck socks, bags and accessories (€9.99–€64), made to order once 100 people reserve an item for €4.99.

## What the board said

**Vote: 2 PASS, 1 FUND IF. Average score 3.3 / 10** (Offers 3 PASS, Monopoly 3 PASS, Product 4 FUND IF).

Full memos: `board/offers.md`, `board/monopoly.md`, `board/product.md`. Everything was judged from the public site (duckbro.store, read 14 Oct 2026). Costs, reservations and followers are unknown.

### Risks all three members raised

1. **The site's trust signals look staged, and that is the most urgent fix.** All three put it first or second:
   - "Almost Gone", "Restocked" and "Best Seller" badges on items nobody can buy.
   - The banner says "Drop 003 is live" while every product says it has ended.
   - Three 5-star homepage quotes (Marc, Priya, Tom) sit beside "No reviews yet" on every product page.
   - Struck-through "was" prices on a brand that has not sold yet.

   For a brand whose only moat is trust and voice, these are self-inflicted holes. In the EU, earlier-price claims and reviews are regulated (the Omnibus Directive rules on price reductions and consumer reviews). A lawyer should confirm, but this is a real risk, not just a style point.
2. **The pre-order asks a lot and promises little.** The buyer pays €4.99 and waits for 100 strangers. There is no date, no progress counter and no stated refund if 100 is never reached.
3. **No focus and no niche.** Twelve SKUs across socks, bags, caps, scarves and charms split early demand, so each item is less likely to reach its own 100 threshold. "Humans who don't take themselves too seriously" is a mood, not a market.
4. **A copyable commodity.** Novelty socks and totes are a crowded category. The duck design and the voice are the only differences.

### Where they disagreed

- **Offers** says the market has almost no pain. To fund it, rebuild one offer around a real occasion (a gift with a delivery-date promise and a "they don't laugh, money back" guarantee) at full price, not a markdown.
- **Monopoly** wants one narrow niche duckbro can own first, proven by 100 reservations coming from that niche.
- **Product** is the most positive. The voice is genuinely good and consistent, and the one-line pitch works. Cut to one hero product and design the whole reservation experience.

### Conditions checklist

- [ ] Remove every unearned signal: stock badges on unavailable items, "was" prices never charged, and homepage quotes not from verified buyers. Fix the banner.
- [ ] Pre-order terms on the page: a deadline, an automatic refund of the €4.99 if 100 is not reached, and a public counter.
- [ ] One hero product for the next drop (the board points to a sock pack)
- [ ] Unit cost, supplier, MOQ and a production sample confirmed (`/founder-cfo`)
- [ ] 100 paid reservations as proof of demand before more spend
- [ ] Name the niche or occasion (e.g. the gift buyer before Christmas) and measure what one reservation costs to win

### The strongest version the board can see

**A gift brand, not a fashion house.** One hero product: a duck sock gift box at full price, with a clear "arrives before Christmas, or your money back" promise. Reservations get a public counter and a refund rule. Every claim on the site is true. The voice is the asset, so spend it on TikTok and Instagram rather than on fake urgency.

## Pricing

Price tested: the Quack Pack (5 pairs), €29.99. Full curve in `pricing-curve.md`.

### What the panel said
- **Acceptable range: €12.10 to €31.99.** Median answers: too cheap €10, a bargain €18, getting expensive €30, too expensive €40.
- €29.99 is **inside** the range but at its top edge, right at the median "getting expensive" point. The indifference price (as many call it a bargain as call it expensive) is about **€22**.
- **Price is not why people said no.** 0 of 20 passed on price. 15 passed on timing (no delivery date) and 5 on trust.

### What the business needs
Unknown. No unit cost, supplier or MOQ is published, so the margin at any price can't be computed. Run `/founder-cfo` with the real landed cost of a Quack Pack before changing the price.

### Recommendation
1. **Keep €29.99 as the real, full price** and drop the struck-through €44.99. The panel accepts €29.99 without an anchor. A "was" price that was never charged is a legal risk in the EU (Omnibus price-indication rules; confirm with a lawyer) and a trust risk the board flagged.
2. **The ladder** (good / better / best), so there's an entry under the €22 indifference point:
   - single pair (Le Waddle) about €9.99–12.99;
   - Quack Pack €29.99;
   - gift box (Quack Pack + Duck Charm + card) about €39.99, just under the €40 "too expensive" median and over the €50 free-shipping line when paired with a second item.
3. **Opening offer:** a founding-flock price for the first 100 reservations only, ending on a fixed date, instead of a permanent "% off".
4. **What to test with real buyers:** €24.99 vs €29.99 for the Quack Pack on two reservation pages with the same delivery date shown. Measure paid reservations, not clicks.

### The panel's price objections, in their words
- "€29.99 is a lot for socks from an unknown brand when ASOS or AliExpress sell duck socks for far less." (P019)
- "He also thinks €30 is a lot for socks when Primark sells funny ones for €5." (P016, summarised)
- "A cheaper single pair to try would also help." (P006)
