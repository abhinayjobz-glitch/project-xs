# Summary

**What it is:** duckbro is a pre-launch EU/UK online brand selling funny duck socks, bags and accessories (€9.99–€64), made to order once 100 people reserve an item for €4.99.

**Verdict: Incomplete.** There are no cost numbers yet, so profit can't be computed. The evidence so far says **not yet**:
- The board voted **2 PASS, 1 FUND IF** (average 3.3/10).
- **0 of 20** simulated buyers would buy as the site stands today.

**Where the board and the panel agree:** the product and the price aren't the problem. The buying experience is.
- **No delivery date.** "Pay €4.99 and wait for 99 strangers" kills impulse and gift purchases. This was 15 of 20 no's.
- **Trust signals that look staged.** All three board members flagged them: stock badges on items you can't buy, a "Drop 003 is live" banner on a drop that has ended, homepage 5-star quotes next to "No reviews yet", and "was" prices on a brand that hasn't sold yet. 5 of 20 buyers said no on trust.
- €29.99 for the Quack Pack is inside what buyers accept (€12–32).

**Where they differ:** the panel's buyers mostly want it in stock now. The board says to keep made-to-order but make it honest, with a date, a refund rule and a counter.

**Biggest risk:** the joke is copyable, so trust and voice are the only moat. Fake-looking urgency spends that moat before the first sale.

**What it needs to start:** unknown until costs are in. Needed: landed unit cost per item, the supplier's MOQ and lead time, the ad budget, and current reservation count.

**This week:** fix the site's trust problems (list in `site-fixes.md`). Add a ship-by date, an automatic €4.99 refund if 100 isn't reached, and a public counter to the Quack Pack. Then send real traffic to it and count paid reservations.

_The panel is simulated buyers and the board applies published frameworks. Real reservations confirm or overturn them. Not financial, legal or tax advice._
