# Pricing: duckbro

Price tested: the Quack Pack (5 pairs), €29.99. Full curve in `pricing-curve.md`.

## What the panel said
- **Acceptable range: €12.10 to €31.99.** Median answers: too cheap €10, a bargain €18, getting expensive €30, too expensive €40.
- €29.99 is **inside** the range but at its top edge, right at the median "getting expensive" point. The indifference price (as many call it a bargain as call it expensive) is about **€22**.
- **Price is not why people said no.** 0 of 20 passed on price. 15 passed on timing (no delivery date) and 5 on trust.

## What the business needs
Unknown. No unit cost, supplier or MOQ is published, so the margin at any price can't be computed. Run `/founder-cfo` with the real landed cost of a Quack Pack before changing the price.

## Recommendation
1. **Keep €29.99 as the real, full price** and drop the struck-through €44.99. The panel accepts €29.99 without an anchor. A "was" price that was never charged is a legal risk in the EU (Omnibus price-indication rules; confirm with a lawyer) and a trust risk the board flagged.
2. **The ladder** (good / better / best), so there's an entry under the €22 indifference point:
   - single pair (Le Waddle) about €9.99–12.99;
   - Quack Pack €29.99;
   - gift box (Quack Pack + Duck Charm + card) about €39.99, just under the €40 "too expensive" median and over the €50 free-shipping line when paired with a second item.
3. **Opening offer:** a founding-flock price for the first 100 reservations only, ending on a fixed date, instead of a permanent "% off".
4. **What to test with real buyers:** €24.99 vs €29.99 for the Quack Pack on two reservation pages with the same delivery date shown. Measure paid reservations, not clicks.

## The panel's price objections, in their words
- "€29.99 is a lot for socks from an unknown brand when ASOS or AliExpress sell duck socks for far less." (P019)
- "He also thinks €30 is a lot for socks when Primark sells funny ones for €5." (P016, summarised)
- "A cheaper single pair to try would also help." (P006)

_Simulated buyers pick what to test; they are not proof._
